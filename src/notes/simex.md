A response plan is first tested either in an exercise or in an emergency. Exercises are the better place to find out what does not work, but they take weeks to design, they are hard to run well, and the record of what was decided, by whom and when, is easily lost before the report is written.

SIMEX is the platform we built for that work. It follows the WHO Simulation Exercise Manual and the step-by-step design sequence of the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control, and it covers the whole cycle of an exercise.

## Design

Start from one of six exercise templates, each a complete synthetic scenario: sub-national outbreak response, airport point of entry, seaport and ground crossing, national multi-sectoral response (One Health), regional information sharing, and deliberate events at the interface of health and security. Ten guided steps cover the purpose, the objectives and the IHR core capacities they test, the roles, the injects, the decision points and the evaluation criteria. SIMEX checks the design and produces the WHO exercise documents: concept note, exercise plan, master scenario events list, evaluation plan, facilitator guide and participant handbooks.

## Run

The facilitator runs the exercise from one console, in the room or online. Participants join from their own phones or laptops with a private link, without an account, and see only their own role. Every release, action and decision, and the reason given for it, is recorded against the exercise clock.

## Evaluate

Criteria are marked met, late or missing while the exercise runs, and evaluators add their observations. A draft after-action report is organised by objective, for the facilitator to review. The record can be replayed to confirm that nothing in it has changed.

## Where AI fits

AI is optional and stays under the facilitator's control. It can draft exercise content and play roles that no one is available to play, but it only ever proposes: nothing enters the design or the record until the facilitator accepts it.

## What it is not

SIMEX is not a certification, an IHR assessment or a substitute for an experienced facilitator, and it does not replace the WHO tools for designated points of entry. While it is being tested, it is for synthetic scenarios only: no real plans, personal data or case records.

## Taking part in testing

We are looking for teams that plan or run exercises, such as ministries of health, emergency operations centres, point-of-entry authorities and their partners, to run a practice exercise with us and tell us what works, what does not, and what is missing. During testing, we set up each exercise with you; participants join with the link they are sent. To take part, write to [info@presentlab.org](mailto:info@presentlab.org?subject=Testing%20SIMEX).
