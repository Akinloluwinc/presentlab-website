Simulation exercises let a team test an emergency plan before it’s needed. They take weeks to design and are hard to run well, and the record of who decided what, and when, is easily lost before the report is written.

We built SIMEX to help with all of that. It follows the WHO Simulation Exercise Manual and the design steps of the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control, and covers an exercise from design to the draft report.

## Design

You start from one of six exercise templates, each a complete synthetic scenario: sub-national outbreak response, airport point of entry, seaport and ground crossing, national multi-sectoral response (One Health), regional information sharing, and deliberate events where health and security meet. Ten guided steps cover the purpose, the objectives and the IHR core capacities they test, the roles, the injects, the decision points and the evaluation criteria. SIMEX checks the design and produces the WHO exercise documents: concept note, exercise plan, master scenario events list, evaluation plan, facilitator guide and participant handbooks.

## Run

The facilitator runs the exercise from one console, in the room or online. Participants join from their own phones or laptops with a private link. They don’t need an account, and they see only their own role. Every release, action and decision is recorded against the exercise clock, with the reason given for it.

## Evaluate

As the exercise runs, each criterion shows where it stands: met, late, overdue or not yet due. Evaluators add their observations. When play ends, the facilitator records the hot wash and agrees an improvement plan with the table: who owns each action, by when, and how it will be checked. SIMEX then drafts the after-action report from the record, organised by objective, for the facilitator to review and finalise. It can be printed, saved as a PDF or downloaded in Word. The record can be replayed to confirm that nothing in it has changed.

## AI is optional

Every part of SIMEX works without AI, and AI stays off unless the facilitator chooses to use it. If they do, it can draft exercise content, play a role that no one is available to play and draft the report’s narrative. It only proposes: nothing goes into the design, the record or the report until the facilitator accepts it, and the record and the report show what it played or drafted. With AI on, the content each task needs is sent to an outside AI service; during testing, that content is synthetic. With AI off, nothing is sent.

## What it is not

SIMEX isn’t a certification, an IHR assessment or a substitute for an experienced facilitator, and it doesn’t replace the WHO tools for designated points of entry. While it’s being tested, it’s for synthetic scenarios only, with no real plans, personal data or case records.

## Taking part in testing

We’re looking for teams that plan or run exercises, such as ministries of health, emergency operations centres, point-of-entry authorities and their partners. We’ll run a practice exercise with you and ask what works, what doesn’t and what’s missing. During testing we set up each exercise with you, and participants join with the link they’re sent.
