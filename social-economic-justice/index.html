Practice III — Social and Economic Justice

Power shifts in practice, or not at all.

Societies function through inequitable and exclusive structures that produce a growing population of oppressed and minoritised people. We work with the organisations trying to change that — on the operational mechanics, not the declarations.

The practice

Power, not participation

We work with communities, movements, civil society organisations, intermediaries, journalists, academics, businesses, governments and international agencies to amplify alternative and new forms of power.

That means retiring regressive humanitarian terminology, moving past checkbox diversity, and treating structural power imbalance as the design problem rather than a sensitivity to be managed. It also means being specific: locally led development fails when ‘local’ is reduced to nationality in ways that privilege elites, or when funds are routed through intermediaries in ways that outsource power rather than shift it.

What we do

Four lines of work

01

Locally led development

Co-design and prototyping with civil society organisations, communities and ecosystem actors through the Local Leadership Labs — surfacing local priorities, testing new ways of working, and challenging the political, technical and behavioural barriers that stop local civil society being recognised, enabled and resourced as key development actors.

02

The Equity DNA Playbook

A framework built to move organisations beyond checkbox diversity toward addressing the structural power imbalances that produce the outcomes they say they want to change.

03

Power & localisation research

Research-driven advocacy on shifting power to local actors — including the uncomfortable questions about what funders give up, and where localisation risks reproducing the hierarchies it claims to dismantle.

04

Facilitation in hard rooms

Inclusive, trauma-informed and low-literacy facilitation for multi-stakeholder processes — designed for the realities of working with diverse communities in constrained environments, rather than applied as a fixed recipe.

Signature method

The co-design and prototyping journey

A four-phase journey, usable end-to-end for multi-day labs or modularly inside a strategy session, community dialogue or programme design process.

Phase 1

Problem discovery

Surface and unpack the real issues underneath visible symptoms. Problem mapping, problem statements, stakeholder mapping, causal layered analysis, How Might We statements — moving from generic problems to a shared, context-specific understanding of what is at stake and for whom.

Phase 2

Problem definition

Narrow the focus and decide which part of the system to work on first. 10x10 brainstorming, user personas, user journeys — clarifying target users, lived experience and priority leverage points.

Phase 3

Designing solutions

Create early-stage solutions that can be tested quickly and cheaply. Prototyping techniques, assumption mapping, prototype storyboards — surfacing what must be true for an idea to work, and designing prototypes local actors can realistically own.

Phase 4

Testing solutions

Stress-test prototypes in real or simulated conditions and generate usable learning. Role-playing and experiment design, supporting structured feedback and a decision to adapt, scale or stop.

Design with, not for. Adapt the tools to local realities, capacities and power dynamics — never as a fixed recipe.

How we hold ourselves

Values that constrain the work

Local agency & power

We amplify local agency and new forms of power, retiring regressive humanitarian terminology in favour of equity-centred models.

Bias for evidence

We move past the theatre of development toward interventions rooted in implementation science and rigorous evaluation.

Future-facing rigor

Contextual depth paired with strategic foresight — designing for second- and third-order consequences.

Psychological safety

An internal culture modelled on Amy Edmondson’s research, which encourages error-reporting and continuous learning.
